### Q01

**Question**: I just got married last month - can I add my spouse to my medical plan now?

**Status**: answered | **Retrieved**: POL-002, POL-003, POL-005, POL-001

Yes, you can add your spouse to your medical plan outside of open enrollment because marriage is a qualifying life event (POL-002). Here are the key details:

**Qualifying Life Event Window**
Marriage is explicitly listed as a qualifying life event under POL-002, which allows you to make benefit changes outside of the annual open enrollment period. However, you must submit your change request **within 30 days of the marriage** (POL-002). Since your marriage occurred last month, you should act immediately and confirm with People Operations that you are still within that 30-day window.

**How to Add Your Spouse**
Once approved, you would move to an **Employee+Spouse** coverage tier, which is one of the four available coverage tiers across all three medical plan options (POL-003):
- Bronze HDHP (HSA-eligible)
- Silver PPO
- Gold PPO

**HSA Note (if applicable)**
If you are currently enrolled in the Bronze HDHP and have an HSA, note that adding a spouse may affect your HSA contribution tier. The employer seeds $750 for individual coverage and $1,500 for family coverage per year (POL-005). You may want to confirm with People Operations how Employee+Spouse coverage is classified for HSA seeding purposes, as the handbook only specifies individual and family tiers for this benefit.

**Action Required**
Contact People Operations as soon as possible to submit your qualifying life event change, given the 30-day submission deadline from your date of marriage (POL-002).

### Q02

**Question**: How much does the company match on my 401k and when does it vest?

**Status**: answered | **Retrieved**: POL-007, POL-015, POL-012, POL-001

There are two versions of the 401(k) match and vesting terms in effect, depending on the date:

**Now through December 31, 2025** (POL-007 applies):
- **Match:** 100% of the first 3% of your contributions, plus 50% of the next 2% — for a **maximum employer match of 4%**.
- **Vesting:** Graded over three years — one-third vests per year (so fully vested after year 3).

**Starting January 1, 2026** (POL-015 supersedes POL-007 on match and vesting):
- **Match:** 100% of the first 4% of eligible pay — a slightly more generous formula.
- **Vesting:** **Immediate** — the match vests as soon as it is credited to your account.

Additional notes that remain unchanged across both periods (POL-007, confirmed by POL-015):
- You can contribute pre-tax or Roth.
- Auto-enrollment is set at 4% after 60 days of employment.

If you have questions about how the transition will work for your specific account, it's worth confirming the details with People Operations as January 1, 2026 approaches.

**Citations**: POL-007, POL-015

### Q04

**Question**: Can I have both an HSA and a healthcare FSA at the same time?

**Status**: answered | **Retrieved**: POL-006, POL-005, POL-003, POL-004

No, you cannot have both a Healthcare FSA and an HSA at the same time.

Per **POL-006**, "You cannot enroll in a Healthcare FSA and an HSA in the same year."

Additionally, per **POL-005**, the HSA is only available to employees enrolled in the Bronze HDHP plan (**POL-003**). If you are enrolled in that plan and wish to take advantage of the HSA (including the employer seed contribution of $750 for individual or $1,500 for family coverage per **POL-005**), you would not be eligible to also enroll in the Healthcare FSA.

Note that this restriction applies specifically to the **Healthcare FSA**. The handbook does not indicate any similar restriction between an HSA and a **Dependent Care FSA**, but if you have questions about combining those two accounts, you should confirm with People Operations, as the handbook does not explicitly address that combination.
